## **1. Definitions**

This Privacy Policy uses terms from data protection law. The following definitions are intended to make the policy easier to understand.

**a) Personal data**

Personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.

**b) Data subject**

A data subject is an identified or identifiable natural person whose personal data is processed.

**c) Processing**

Processing means any operation performed on personal data, such as collection, recording, organisation, storage, alteration, retrieval, use, disclosure, restriction or erasure.

**d) Restriction of processing**

Restriction of processing means marking stored personal data with the aim of limiting its future processing.

**e) Profiling**

Profiling means automated processing of personal data used to evaluate certain personal aspects of a natural person, such as preferences, interests, behaviour or location.

**f) Pseudonymisation**

Pseudonymisation means processing personal data so that it can no longer be attributed to a specific person without additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately and protected.

**g) Controller**

The controller is the natural or legal person or other body that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data.

**h) Processor**

A processor is a natural or legal person or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the controller.

**i) Recipient**

A recipient is a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body to which personal data is disclosed.

**j) Third party**

A third party is a person or body other than the data subject, controller, processor or persons authorised to process personal data under the direct authority of the controller or processor.

**k) Consent**

Consent means a freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of the data subject’s wishes by which they agree to the processing of their personal data.

## **6. Rights of data subjects under the GDPR**

**a) Right to confirmation**

You have the right to ask us to confirm whether personal data concerning you is being processed.

**b) Right of access**

You have the right to obtain information about the personal data we store about you and to receive a copy of that data. This includes, where applicable, information about processing purposes, data categories, recipients, retention periods, your rights to rectification or erasure, your right to lodge a complaint, the source of data not collected directly from you, and information about automated decision-making.

You may also ask whether personal data has been transferred to a third country or international organisation and, where applicable, request information about the safeguards used.

**c) Right to rectification**

You have the right to request the correction of inaccurate personal data and the completion of incomplete data.

**d) Right to erasure**

You have the right to request the erasure of personal data where the conditions laid down in Article 17 GDPR are met, for example where the data is no longer necessary for the purposes for which it was collected, consent is withdrawn and no other legal basis applies, processing is unlawful, or erasure is required by law.

**e) Right to restriction of processing**

You may request restriction of processing where the conditions set out in Article 18 GDPR are met, for example while the accuracy of data is being checked or where you object to processing and the balancing of interests is still pending.

**f) Right to data portability**

Where the legal requirements are met, you have the right to receive personal data you have provided to us in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and to transmit it to another controller.

**g) Right to object**

You have the right to object, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to processing based on Article 6(1)(e) or (f) GDPR. We will stop processing unless we demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or the processing is needed for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.

**h) Automated individual decision-making, including profiling**

You have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, where it produces legal effects concerning you or similarly significantly affects you, subject to the exceptions set out in Article 22 GDPR.

**i) Right to withdraw consent**

Where processing is based on consent, you may withdraw your consent at any time. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before withdrawal.

To exercise these rights, please contact us using the contact details in Section 2.

## **10. Retention periods**

Personal data is retained for the applicable statutory retention period or, where no statutory period applies, for as long as necessary for the relevant purpose. Once the purpose has ceased and any applicable statutory retention periods have expired, the data is deleted unless another lawful basis requires further retention.

The following retention periods apply to OMSI Mod Hub:

- **Access logs:** deleted no later than 14 days after collection.
- **Rate-limit data:** deleted after 24 hours. Rate-limit data maintained by OMSI Mod Hub itself stores IP addresses in hashed form. The authentication system may also process IP addresses in plain text for security and abuse prevention.
- **Session data and logged-in device entries:** deleted upon logout or no later than 30 days.
- **Unconfirmed accounts:** deleted 7 days after registration if the email address has not been confirmed.
- **Verification, password-reset and email-change codes or links:** deleted or invalidated as soon as they expire.
- **Closed reports:** retained for 6 months after closure. Open reports remain stored until they have been processed.
- **Moderation and administration log entries:** retained for 12 months.
- **User accounts and associated content:** retained until the user deletes the account, subject to any further retention required by law or necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.
- **Backups:** deleted data may remain in daily backups for up to 14 days. If additional server backups operated by Hetzner are enabled, data may remain there for up to a further 7 days.

These are the intended standard retention periods. If data must be retained longer in a specific case to comply with legal obligations or investigate a specific security incident, it will be retained only to the extent and for the period necessary.
